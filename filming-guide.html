Roof Guardians Filming Guide
Six videos, one session

Read it, say it your way, keep it moving

Every word you need is on this page. Six short videos, shot on your phone, in roughly an hour. Open this on the phone you are filming with, or print it. Nothing here has to be memorised.

Before you press record

Six rules. They matter more than the words do.

1
Hold the phone upright, not sideways.Vertical fills the screen on Facebook and Instagram. Sideways video gets shrunk into a strip and scrolled past.
2
Start on the first word.No hello, no introduction, no "so today I want to talk about". First sentence, straight in. You can trim the front later, you cannot add attention back.
3
Get close, and get out of the wind.Phone at arm's length or closer, and if it is gusty, film in the lee of the house or in the truck. Bad audio kills a video faster than bad picture.
4
Film where the work is.On a roof, next to the truck, standing in a driveway looking up. Anywhere but a desk. The background is doing half the selling.
5
Fluff a line and just restart the sentence.Keep rolling. We cut it. Two or three takes of each one is plenty, and the second take is usually the good one.
6
Say it the way you would say it to a customer.These are written in plain speech on purpose. Change any word that does not sound like you. The point is that it is you, not that it is the script.
Anything highlighted in yellow is a blank for you to fill in with a number or a fact only you know. If you are not certain of the figure, leave it out of the sentence entirely rather than rounding it.

The six videos

One subject each. Say the whole thing in one take if you can. Every script below runs between 25 and 50 seconds at a normal speaking pace.

1

Three signs a roof is at the end

40 sec
Priority
Say this

If your roof is fifteen years or older, here are three things worth checking before you pay for another repair.

One. Granules. If the bottom of your gutters looks like it has sand in it, your shingles are shedding the layer that protects them.

Two. The shingles themselves. Once they start curling at the corners, or you can see bald patches, they are past the point of sealing back down.

Three. Repairs that keep coming back. If the same area has been patched twice, the problem is usually not that spot. It is the system around it.

None of that means you need a new roof tomorrow. It means somebody should get up there and show you photographs instead of a price.

Where to stand

In a driveway with a house behind you, or on a roof if you are already up there. Sunlight behind the camera, not behind you.

Grab afterwards

Close ups of granules in a gutter, a curling shingle corner, and a patched area. We cut these in over your voice.

Optional last line: "If yours is getting up there in age, tap below and we will come and photograph it."
2

The ceiling stain lies

35 sec
Priority
Say this

When you get a stain on your ceiling, the leak is almost never directly above it.

Water gets in somewhere, runs along the decking or down a rafter, and it only shows itself where it finally drips through. That can be feet away from where it started.

So if somebody goes up, patches the area over the stain and tells you it is handled, be careful. If they never found where the water actually got in, it comes back with the next rain.

When we inspect a leak we trace it back to the entry point, and we photograph it, so you can see the same thing we saw.

Where to stand

Indoors under a stained ceiling if you have access to one, otherwise outside pointing up at a valley or a pipe boot.

Grab afterwards

A ceiling stain from below, a cracked pipe boot, lifted flashing, and any decking photo showing a water track.

Optional last line: "If you have a stain and nobody has explained where it came from, tap below."
3

What we document before an adjuster arrives

40 sec
Claims
Say this

If you are filing a claim on your roof, the thing that helps you most happens before anyone from the insurance company shows up.

We photograph every slope. We mark the hits. We take close ups with a measurement in the frame, and we note the date of the storm.

So when the adjuster does come out, there is a record the two of us can walk through together, instead of two people standing in a yard squinting at a roof and disagreeing about what is up there.

We do not decide your claim. Your carrier does that. Our job is to make sure the condition of your roof is documented properly before anybody makes a decision about it.

Where to stand

Beside the truck with equipment visible, or on a roof mid inspection with a chalk mark or measuring tape in shot.

Grab afterwards

A phone or tablet showing a photo report, chalk circles on hail hits, a tape measure against a bruise.

Optional last line: "If you have storm damage and no documentation yet, tap below and we will get it on record."
4

Solar panels and a roof replacement

35 sec
Your lane
Say this

If you have solar on your roof and the roof itself needs replacing, there is a step most homeowners have never heard of. It is called a detach and reset.

The panels come off, the roof gets done properly underneath, and the panels go back on afterwards.

What actually matters is who is coordinating it. When the roofing company and the solar company are not talking to each other, you end up in the middle of an argument about penetrations and warranties, and it is your roof either way.

We handle the coordination, so one company is accountable for everything that happens up there.

Where to stand

In front of a house with panels on the roof if you have one on the schedule. If not, the driveway works.

Grab afterwards

Panels on a roof, a mount or rail close up, and any photo of a detach and reset in progress.

Optional last line: "Solar on the roof and a roof that is due? Tap below and we will plan both together."
5

What we actually look for up there

40 sec
Evergreen
Say this

People ask what we are really doing when we get on a roof, so here it is. Four things.

The field of the roof. The shingles themselves, and whether there is life left in them.

The penetrations. Vents, pipes, anything cut into the roof, because that is where most leaks actually start.

The flashing. The valleys, and everywhere the roof meets a wall or a chimney.

And the decking underneath, wherever we can get eyes on it.

Then you get photographs of all of it, whether or not there is any work to do. Plenty of the roofs we look at do not need anything yet, and we will tell you that.

Where to stand

On a roof, ideally walking slowly while you talk. Movement holds attention better than standing still.

Grab afterwards

One shot of each of the four: field, a pipe boot, a valley or wall flashing, and exposed decking.

Optional last line: "Want to know where yours stands? Tap below and we will come and look."
6

Who we are and why we photograph everything

30 sec
Trust
Say this

Your name, Texas Roof Guardians, here in San Antonio.

Here is the one thing I want you to know about how we work. We photograph everything. Every inspection, every slope, before and after.

Not because it looks impressive, but because when you are dealing with a roof, and often with an insurance company at the same time, the person holding the documentation is the one who gets taken seriously.

You will never get a number out of us without seeing exactly what it is based on.

Where to stand

In front of the branded truck, or on a job with the crew working behind you. This is the one where the background should say who you are.

Grab afterwards

The truck, a yard sign, the crew working, and a wide shot of a finished roof.

Optional last line: "If you want that kind of look at your roof, tap below."

While you are out there

Silent footage, no talking needed. Five to ten seconds each. This is what we cut over your voice, and it is worth as much as the talking is.

  • Damage close ups. Hail bruising, lifted or missing shingles, cracked pipe boots, failed flashing, granules in a gutter.
  • The work happening. Tear off, decking going down, shingles being laid, the crew moving.
  • Before and after from the same spot. Stand in the same place both times. This is the single most effective thing on the list.
  • Company shots. The truck, a yard sign, the crew, a clean finished roof from the street.
  • Drone footage. Anything you already have, we will use it.
Sending it over. Drop everything into one Google Drive or Dropbox folder and share the link. Files sent by text message or WhatsApp get compressed on the way and arrive too low quality to run as ads. Film vertically, and leave the phone on its normal highest quality setting.
